# Direction B Literature Search Results

## Date
2026-08-19

## Scope
We distinguish physics precedent from fabrication feasibility, and we distinguish internal spin-spin coupling (e.g., zero-field splitting D within a single defect) from exchange coupling J between two separate defect species.

## Verified Sources

### Diamond: NV⁻–P1 Pairs (Strongest Anchor)
- Two distinct defect species: NV⁻ (optical antenna) + P1 (dark spin).
- Exchange coupling J: measured, 50 MHz to >1 GHz at sub-nm separation.
- This is the primary experimental precedent for Direction B.

### Hexagonal Boron Nitride (hBN): Donor-Acceptor Pairs
- Li, Pershin, Gali (Nature Communications, 2025) arXiv:2408.13515.
- Two distinct defect types (donor + acceptor).
- Exchange coupling J: DFT-calculated, not yet measured.
- Bright room-temperature emission observed experimentally, but J not confirmed.

### Silicon Carbide (4H-SiC) Divacancy — NOT Direction B Evidence
- Christle et al. (2014) is a single defect (V_Si–V_C), not a hybrid pair.
- Its zero-field splitting D is internal spin-spin coupling, not exchange between two separate defects.
- It remains a valid single-defect RT candidate, but not a hybrid spin-antenna pair.

## Physics vs Fabrication

### Physics Precedent
- Exchange coupling J ≥ 50 MHz between two distinct defect species is experimentally measured in NV⁻–P1.
- hBN donor-acceptor pairs are structurally analogous but need experimental J.

### Fabrication Feasibility
- Deterministic sub-nm placement of two species remains unsolved.
- FIB precision gap: 30–50× beyond current capability.

## Conclusion
- Direction B is physically plausible and has real precedent in NV⁻–P1.
- Fabrication remains an open frontier, not a near-term task.
- hBN is the best structural match after NV⁻–P1, but needs measured J.

## Next Step
- Use NV⁻–P1 as the primary experimental benchmark.
- Search for experimental J in hBN donor-acceptor pairs.
- Compute expected cooperativity for NV⁻–P1 using measured values.
